Guide · 6 min read

How to launch a brokerage in 2026

Launching a brokerage looks complicated from the outside, but the order of work is simple: licence, platform, liquidity, CRM, payments. Get the order wrong and you pay for it — usually by rebuilding something you already bought.

1. Choose your model first

Are you an A-book broker, a B-book broker, or a hybrid? Do you target retail FX traders, or a niche like gold or crypto? The model decides your licence route, your liquidity needs and your risk setup. Decide this before you buy anything.

2. Sort the licence route

Most jurisdictions require a licence to operate a brokerage. Work with legal advisers on the route that fits your model and markets, and plan your launch timeline around it — licences take longer than technology.

3. Pick the stack, not the parts

A platform without liquidity is a demo. A CRM without payments is a spreadsheet. The fastest launches buy the stack as one integrated setup — platform, liquidity, CRM, risk and payments — from a single team. That's how BrokerForge launches go live in 2–4 weeks.

4. Launch small, scale deliberately

Open with the symbols and markets your first clients actually trade. Add instruments, platforms and providers as volumes grow. Talk to us and we'll scope a launch plan for your model.

Platforms · 5 min read

MT5-type vs cTrader: which fits your broker?

Two platforms dominate the shortlist for new brokers, and they attract different traders. The right choice depends on who you're serving, not which one is "better" in the abstract.

MT5-type platforms

The default choice for most brokers. Traders know it, EAs and indicators are everywhere, and it covers FX, metals, indices and crypto in one terminal. If your market is broad retail FX, this is usually the safe first platform.

cTrader

Cleaner, faster-feeling, and popular with traders who care about depth of market and a modern interface. A strong choice if your audience skews toward active, tech-comfortable traders — and a good second platform to add later.

The practical answer

Many brokers run both. Start with the platform your first clients already use, then add the other as you grow. See the full line-up on our platforms page.

Operations · 4 min read

What good brokerage support looks like

Traders remember two things about a broker: how fast withdrawals arrive, and whether anyone answers when something breaks at 2am. Support is a retention product, not a cost centre.

Answer fast, escalate clearly

First response should be minutes, not hours — and the person answering should know when to pull in an engineer instead of guessing. Our support runs 24/7 with real engineers behind the front line.

Prevent, don't just fix

Monitoring catches most issues before clients notice: feed health, server load, failed deposits. The best support ticket is the one that never gets opened. That's how we run support at BrokerForge — more about us.

Liquidity · 5 min read

Liquidity explained: spreads, depth and routing

"Liquidity" gets thrown around a lot. In practice it answers one question: when your client clicks buy, how good is the price they get — and how much size can they trade at that price?

Aggregation beats a single source

A single provider can widen spreads or thin out at exactly the wrong moment. Aggregating several providers means the best bid and ask usually comes from whoever is sharpest right now — and depth from all of them combined.

Routing matters as much as price

Smart routing sends each order to the provider offering the best fill for that size and symbol. Good routing is invisible; bad routing shows up as slippage your clients can feel. See how our liquidity and bridge work.

CRM · 4 min read

Why your CRM is your growth engine

Most brokers buy a platform first and think about the CRM later. That's backwards. The CRM is where onboarding, KYC, IBs, deposits and retention actually happen — it's where growth is won or lost.

Onboarding is the first product

A client who can't register, verify and deposit in one smooth flow is a client you lose before they trade. Your CRM should make that flow obvious — for the client and for your compliance team.

IBs compound

Partners bring traders, traders bring volume, volume brings more partners. A CRM that tracks IB trees, rebates and payouts accurately is what keeps that flywheel spinning. See our Forex CRM — plans start from $500.

Prop trading · 5 min read

Starting a prop firm: challenges that actually work

Prop firms live or die on trust: traders must believe the rules are fair, the dashboard is accurate, and payouts actually arrive. Get those three right and the rest is marketing.

Rules traders can understand

Keep challenge rules simple and visible — profit target, drawdown, daily loss. If a trader needs a lawyer to understand why they failed, your rules are the problem.

Payouts on time, every time

Nothing kills a prop firm faster than slow payouts. Build the payout workflow — request, approval, payment — before you sell your first challenge. See our prop trading solutions.
